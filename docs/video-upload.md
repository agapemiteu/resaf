## Description

The last item is available, but you can't buy it. An agent keeps renewing its hold. Resaf is an AI reservation agent businesses and developers can connect to their reservations, backed by verified PayPal commitments and merchant-controlled rules.

Two real AI shoppers compete for one parking space. Gemini chooses structured tools, and AG Grid exposes the competing requests. A confirmed $2 PayPal commitment activates the paid reservation. A separate $25 booking completes, and the accepted business policy returns the $2 commitment through a confirmed PayPal refund. The displayed provider identifiers belong to the same recorded Sandbox round.

The business sets fees, deadlines and refund terms. Resaf enforces inventory and timing; PayPal supplies the financial state it verifies. Future commitment fees can rise or fall under configured rules while accepted prices and refund promises stay fixed. An unpaid claim that expires gives another buyer a chance.

Quantum Metric's 2026 benchmark reports 48 abandoned retail-agent carts per purchase versus about three overall. Adobe forecasts $275.1 billion in U.S. online holiday sales. For a parking operator, ten additional completed $25 bookings per day could generate $7,500 in monthly booking revenue over 30 days. This projected scenario shows the opportunity: turn blocked availability into completed bookings.

Businesses and developers can connect Resaf to parking, hotel rooms, appointments, services and scarce products. Agents move faster. They shouldn't shut everyone else out.

All funds are PayPal Sandbox test money and the inventory is a demonstration resource. The full original laptop screen is preserved with localized privacy blur. Narration is AI-generated.
